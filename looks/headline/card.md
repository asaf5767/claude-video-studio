pace: Fast and punchy. A cut, punch-in or headline change roughly every 1-1.5 seconds -- constant forward motion.
transitions: Hard cuts between beats; a quick punch-in zoom (scale 1.00 to about 1.08) on emphasis words and on most cuts to add impact.
graphics: The big headline above the head is the main graphic; keep supporting icons or call-outs minimal so they don't compete with it. A bold color block or simple shape can sit behind the headline for contrast.
music: Energetic and driving -- a beat that pushes the pace, present throughout, mixed to leave room for the voice and the whoosh/punch SFX.
sfx: A whoosh on every cut and headline change, a punchier hit SFX on each punch-in zoom. Sound should feel as fast as the edit.
hook: The headline slams in on frame one with a whoosh as the subject starts speaking directly under it -- visual and audio land together.
avoid: Slow holds, soft fades, muted or desaturated colors, long multi-line captions, letting the headline sit over the subject's face or eyes.

Use this look when the creator wants maximum energy and retention -- hooks, countdowns, "here's why" explainers, anything built to stop the scroll and keep moving. It is the loudest and fastest of the four looks.

Practical notes for editing:
- The headline above the head is the signature move: update its text at nearly every beat change so it always names what's being said right now, like a running chapter title. Keep it short (1-4 words) so it reads instantly.
- Never let the headline or its color block cover the subject's eyes or mouth -- raise or shrink it if their head position in frame runs high.
- Punch-ins should land exactly on the stressed word or the cut point, not drift a few frames late -- that's what makes the energy feel intentional rather than jittery.
- Word-by-word captions and the headline are two different layers; don't restate the headline's words in the captions at the same instant, and don't run both for more than a beat or two without a cut.
- If the source footage is calm or slow, this look still works by adding energy through cuts, punch-ins and headline changes -- ask for more/shorter clips from the creator if the only footage available is one long static take.
